WHAT IS YOGA?

Yoga is an ancient practice that originated in India thousands of years ago. It is a holistic science of life, not just a form of exercise. Yoga combines physical postures (asanas), breathing techniques (pranayama), meditation (dhyana), and mental discipline to promote overall physical, mental, emotional, and spiritual well-being.

The word “yoga” comes from the Sanskrit root yuj, which means “to yoke” or “to unite.” This represents the union of body, mind, and consciousness, helping an individual achieve harmony within themselves and with the surrounding world.

According to the Yoga Sutras of Patanjali, yoga is defined as
“Yogah Chitta Vritti Nirodhah,” meaning yoga is the control or cessation of the fluctuations of the mind. This highlights that yoga is not only physical but deeply mental and spiritual.

WHAT YOGA DOES

Yoga improves flexibility, builds muscle strength, corrects posture, protects the spine, and enhances bone health. It increases blood circulation, improves lymphatic drainage, and boosts immunity. Regular yoga practice supports better digestion, respiration, and hormonal balance.

Mentally, yoga improves focus and concentration, calms the nervous system, enhances balance and coordination, and promotes deeper and better sleep. Emotionally, it increases self-awareness, patience, and emotional stability.

HOW HEALTHY YOGA KEEPS US IF PRACTICED DAILY

Practicing yoga daily can have powerful and long-lasting effects on health.

From a physical health perspective, yoga helps lower blood pressure, regulate blood sugar levels, reduce the risk of heart disease, manage body weight, and improve digestion and metabolism.

From a mental health perspective, daily yoga significantly reduces stress, anxiety, and depression. It promotes mindfulness, improves emotional control, and enhances mental clarity.

In the long term, consistent yoga practice is associated with slower aging, healthier joints, improved lung capacity, stronger immunity, and better overall quality of life.

HOW DO NORMAL PEOPLE START YOGA?

Yoga is simple to begin and does not require special equipment.

First, find a quiet and clean space with enough room to stretch comfortably.
Second, use a non-slip yoga mat if available, though a towel or carpet can also work for beginners.
Third, start with basic poses such as Mountain Pose, Child’s Pose, and gentle stretching movements.
Fourth, always listen to your body. Yoga should never cause sharp pain; discomfort means you should stop or modify the pose.
Fifth, focus on slow, deep breathing through the nose.
Finally, be consistent. Even 10 to 15 minutes of daily practice is more beneficial than long sessions done irregularly.

AT WHAT TIME SHOULD WE DO YOGA?

The best time to practice yoga is early morning, ideally before sunrise, during Brahma Muhurta. At this time, the air is fresh, the stomach is empty, and the mind is calm and focused.

However, yoga can be practiced at any time of the day.
Morning yoga energizes the body and prepares you for the day.
Evening yoga helps release stress, reduce fatigue, and promote restful sleep.

Yoga should not be practiced immediately after heavy meals.

BEST TIME: BEFORE BREAKFAST, LUNCH, OR DINNER?

Yoga is best practiced on an empty stomach.

Before breakfast is ideal because the body is light and digestion is not active.
If practicing before lunch or dinner, wait at least 2–3 hours after a heavy meal or about 1 hour after a light snack. Practicing on a full stomach may cause discomfort and affect digestion.

HOW MANY YOGA POSES ARE THERE?

Traditionally, it is believed that there are 8,400,000 asanas, symbolizing the number of living species. However, classical hatha yoga texts such as the Hatha Yoga Pradipika mention only 84 principal poses.

In modern practice, most yoga classes focus on a core set of 20 to 50 commonly used asanas that provide maximum physical and mental benefits.

COMMON YOGA POSES AND THEIR BENEFITS

Mountain Pose (Tadasana) improves posture and stability.
Downward-Facing Dog (Adho Mukha Svanasana) stretches the hamstrings, shoulders, and calves while strengthening the arms.
Warrior I (Virabhadrasana I) strengthens the legs and opens the hips and chest.
Tree Pose (Vrksasana) improves balance and concentration.
Child’s Pose (Balasana) calms the brain and relieves stress.
Cobra Pose (Bhujangasana) strengthens the spine and stretches the chest.
Corpse Pose (Savasana) promotes complete relaxation and is usually performed at the end of practice.

THE 8 LIMBS OF YOGA (ASHTANGA YOGA)

Yoga is a complete system described as an eight-step path.

Yama focuses on moral discipline such as non-violence and truthfulness.
Niyama emphasizes personal discipline like cleanliness and contentment.
Asana refers to physical postures.
Pranayama involves breath control.
Pratyahara is the withdrawal of senses from distractions.
Dharana develops concentration.
Dhyana is meditation.
Samadhi is a state of deep bliss and self-realization.

Most modern yoga focuses mainly on asana and pranayama, but true yoga includes all eight limbs.

PRANAYAMA (BREATHING TECHNIQUES)

Pranayama regulates prana, or life energy.

Anulom Vilom balances the nervous system.
Kapalbhati cleanses the lungs and improves digestion.
Bhastrika increases oxygen supply and energy.
Ujjayi improves focus and endurance.
Bhramari reduces anxiety, anger, and mental tension.

Regular pranayama improves lung capacity, reduces stress hormones, and enhances brain oxygenation.

ASANAS AND THEIR PURPOSE

Asanas are designed to strengthen muscles, massage internal organs, improve flexibility, and activate endocrine glands.

Standing poses build balance and strength.
Seated poses improve flexibility and digestion.
Forward bends calm the mind.
Backward bends increase energy and confidence.
Twists help detoxify the body.
Inversions improve blood circulation to the brain.
Restorative poses promote healing and deep relaxation.

MENTAL AND EMOTIONAL BENEFITS OF YOGA

Yoga directly affects the autonomic nervous system by activating the parasympathetic “rest and digest” response. It reduces cortisol levels, improves emotional regulation, enhances memory and attention, and supports trauma recovery.

Yoga is widely used as therapy for anxiety disorders, depression, PTSD, insomnia, and ADHD.

YOGA FOR DISEASE MANAGEMENT

Yoga is used as a preventive and complementary therapy. It helps manage diabetes, hypertension, obesity, asthma, arthritis, back pain, PCOS, and heart disease. Yoga supports healing but does not replace medical treatment.

IDEAL DURATION AND FREQUENCY

Beginners can start with 10–20 minutes per day.
Intermediate practitioners benefit from 30–45 minutes daily.
Advanced practitioners may practice for 60–90 minutes.

Consistency matters more than duration.

WHO CAN PRACTICE YOGA?

Yoga can be practiced by children, adults, elderly people, athletes, office workers, pregnant women (with prenatal yoga), and people with disabilities using modified poses. Yoga adapts to the individual.


HOW MANY YOGA POSES (ASANAS) ARE THERE?

Traditionally, yoga texts state that there are 8,400,000 asanas, symbolizing all living species. This number represents the infinite variety of postures the human body can express.

However, classical yoga literature such as the Hatha Yoga Pradipika mentions 84 main asanas, out of which 32 are considered most useful for the human body.

In modern yoga practice, most teachers and classes focus on 20 to 50 essential poses, which provide maximum health benefits and are safe for daily practice.

BASIC WAY TO PRACTICE ANY YOGA POSE

Before doing any asana, follow these general rules:

Practice on an empty stomach

Wear comfortable clothing

Move slowly and mindfully

Coordinate movement with breathing

Never force a pose

Stop if there is sharp pain

End practice with relaxation (Savasana)

COMMON YOGA POSES – HOW TO DO THEM & BENEFITS
MOUNTAIN POSE (TADASANA)

How to do:
Stand straight with feet together.
Distribute body weight evenly on both feet.
Relax shoulders and keep arms by the side.
Take deep breaths and stretch the spine upward.

Benefits:
Improves posture and balance.
Strengthens legs and core.
Increases body awareness.

DOWNWARD-FACING DOG (ADHO MUKHA SVANASANA)

How to do:
Start on hands and knees.
Lift hips upward forming an inverted “V” shape.
Keep heels pressing toward the floor and head relaxed.

Benefits:
Stretches hamstrings, calves, and shoulders.
Strengthens arms and legs.
Improves blood circulation.

TREE POSE (VRKSASANA)

How to do:
Stand on one leg and place the other foot on the inner thigh or calf.
Bring palms together at the chest.
Focus on a fixed point and breathe steadily.

Benefits:
Improves balance and concentration.
Strengthens legs.
Enhances focus and mental stability.

COBRA POSE (BHUJANGASANA)

How to do:
Lie on your stomach with palms under shoulders.
Inhale and lift chest using back muscles.
Keep elbows slightly bent and shoulders relaxed.

Benefits:
Strengthens spine.
Improves lung capacity.
Relieves back pain and fatigue.

CHILD’S POSE (BALASANA)

How to do:
Kneel on the floor and sit back on heels.
Lower forehead to the mat with arms extended or relaxed.
Breathe deeply.

Benefits:
Relieves stress and fatigue.
Calms the mind.
Relaxes back and neck muscles.

CORPSE POSE (SAVASANA)

How to do:
Lie flat on your back.
Keep arms and legs relaxed.
Close eyes and breathe naturally.

Benefits:
Deep relaxation.
Reduces stress and anxiety.
Integrates benefits of practice.

WHAT ARE SURYA ASANAS?

Surya Asanas are the 12 postures of Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation).
Surya Namaskar is a complete yoga sequence that combines asana, pranayama, and meditation.

It is traditionally performed in the morning while facing the sun.

12 STEPS OF SURYA NAMASKAR (SURYA ASANAS)
1. PRANAMASANA (Prayer Pose)

Stand straight, palms together.

Benefits:
Improves focus and calmness.

2. HASTA UTTANASANA (Raised Arms Pose)

Raise arms and bend slightly backward.

Benefits:
Improves spinal flexibility and breathing.

3. HASTA PADASANA (Hand to Foot Pose)

Bend forward and touch feet.

Benefits:
Improves digestion and stretches spine.

4. ASHWA SANCHALANASANA (Equestrian Pose)

Step one leg back, look forward.

Benefits:
Strengthens legs and opens hips.

5. DANDASANA (Plank Pose)

Body in straight line, arms strong.

Benefits:
Strengthens arms, shoulders, and core.

6. ASHTANGA NAMASKARA (Eight-Limbed Pose)

Knees, chest, and chin touch floor.

Benefits:
Strengthens chest and arms.

7. BHUJANGASANA (Cobra Pose)

Lift chest upward.

Benefits:
Strengthens spine and lungs.

8. ADHO MUKHA SVANASANA

Lift hips forming inverted V.

Benefits:
Improves circulation and flexibility.

9. ASHWA SANCHALANASANA

Bring opposite leg forward.

Benefits:
Improves balance and strength.

10. HASTA PADASANA

Bend forward again.

Benefits:
Massages abdominal organs.

11. HASTA UTTANASANA

Raise arms and bend backward.

Benefits:
Expands chest and lungs.

12. PRANAMASANA

Return to prayer position.

Benefits:
Balances body and mind.

BENEFITS OF SURYA NAMASKAR

Improves flexibility and strength.
Boosts metabolism and aids weight loss.
Improves digestion and blood circulation.
Balances hormones.
Enhances mental focus and calmness.
Strengthens cardiovascular system.

HOW MANY SURYA NAMASKARS SHOULD WE DO?

Beginners: 4–6 rounds
Intermediate: 8–12 rounds
Advanced: 20+ rounds

Always increase gradually.

SAFETY PRECAUTIONS

Avoid Surya Namaskar if you have:

Severe back pain

Recent surgery

High blood pressure (advanced versions)

Pregnancy (without expert guidance)
